What stands out

Verification is the bridge between adoption and trust.

The most common trust position is conditional, not absolute. Developers appear comfortable delegating a first pass while keeping the right to test, compare and reject.

What not to conclude

High usage does not equal blind confidence.

These results do not show that developers want AI to make consequential decisions. Only 6.6% selected the broadest trust option.

Recommendation

Design for verification,
not autopilot.

Teams introducing AI tools should make checking the output easier than trusting it blindly. Put context, tests and an undo path beside the suggestion—not in a separate process people will skip.

  1. 01
    Start with reversible work

    Use AI first where a mistake is visible and easy to undo.

  2. 02
    Bring proof into the workflow

    Show source context, tests and security checks next to the output.

  3. 03
    Keep accountability human

    For important decisions, name the person who reviews and approves.

Methods & limits

Read the chart with the sample in mind.

Dataset

Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2026

The full survey reports 30,903 valid responses across 169 countries. This analysis uses published aggregates from the AI section, where question-level samples are smaller and vary by view.

Representation

Respondents, not every developer

This is a voluntary survey of Stack Overflow’s developer community. Results describe respondents and should not be treated as a census of all software professionals.

Interpretation

Association, not cause

The survey captures self-reported attitudes and behavior at one point in time. It cannot show that one behavior caused another.
